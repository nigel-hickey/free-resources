# Kitchen Inventory Starter

Your AI can know what's in your refrigerator. And your pantry, and your freezer. This is the starter kit for building that with whatever AI you already use.

I built my own version because I could never remember what I actually had. I'd buy something I already owned, or find out mid cook that I'd used the last of something I needed. Six weeks in, mine still runs my kitchen. I wrote the whole story here, including the parts that went wrong: [BLOG LINK: add when the post is live]

This folder is the setup I'd hand a friend who wanted to do the same thing.

## What you need

Any AI chat app on your phone. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, whatever is already installed. No smart fridge, no new accounts, no spreadsheet skills. About thirty minutes the first time, most of it standing in your kitchen talking.

One honest note before you start: I'm not promising this works exactly the same for you. This is how I did it, and you can do something similar with the tools you've already got. It has clunky manual parts. Mine still does. The habit matters a lot more than the AI does.

## What's in this folder

- **starter-prompt.md**: the instructions you give your AI so it knows the job and the rules.
- **pantry-inventory-template.md**: the file that holds what's actually in your kitchen.
- **shopping-list-template.md**: the list your AI rebuilds for you after every cook.

## Setup, step by step

1. **Give your AI the job.** Open a new chat and paste in everything from `starter-prompt.md`. If your AI app supports projects or custom instructions, put it there instead so you never have to paste it again.
2. **Walk your kitchen and talk.** Use the voice button and read your shelves out loud, in the order they actually sit: fridge, freezer, pantry, shelf by shelf. Nobody is typing thirty packets and jars by hand on a phone. Voice is what makes this possible. Ask the AI to organize what you said into the structure from `pantry-inventory-template.md`.
3. **Save the inventory file.** Download or copy what it built and keep it somewhere you can find it again: your notes app, your files, a cloud drive. That file is now the truth about your kitchen.
4. **Use it when you cook.** Before you start, give the AI your current inventory file and ask what you have and what's missing for the meal you want. After you eat, say one line: deduct this cook from my inventory.
5. **Swap the files.** The AI hands back a rebuilt inventory and an updated shopping list. Download both and replace your old copies. That swap is the whole system. Skip it and things drift.

## The rules I learned the hard way

These are the ones that cost me something to figure out. Start with them on day one.

1. **One file is the truth.** Keep exactly one current copy of your inventory. The moment two copies exist, you will eventually update the wrong one.
2. **Put the date at the top of every version.** It's the fastest way to spot a stale file before it burns you.
3. **Swap the updated file back in before the next conversation.** If you cook in two separate chats without swapping between them, both work off the same outdated file and one quietly overwrites the other.
4. **Make the AI tell you what it's holding before you build on it.** Ask it how many items or recipes are in the file it can see. Mine once showed four recipes when my real collection had ten, and I caught it right before six of them would have quietly disappeared.
5. **Don't let it fake precision.** Countable things get counted. Staples like spices and oil get a plain question instead: did this run low, yes or no. A fair estimate you trust beats an exact number it made up.

## Where you can take it next

Once the inventory and shopping list feel natural, mine grew a recipe collection and a file for my kitchen equipment, and yours can too. Start with the two files here first.
